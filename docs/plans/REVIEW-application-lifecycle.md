# Adversarial review of branch application-lifecycle (2026-10-08)

Reviewer verdict: MERGE AFTER FIXES. Findings 1 to 8 block the merge. 9 to 16 are to be fixed before mail-sync, import or review are relied on. 17 to 20 are low. PROVEN means the reviewer demonstrated it by running code; READ-ONLY means found by reading.

## CRITICAL
1. mailsync.py:28, classify 117-129. PROVEN. A routine acknowledgement is classified as a rejection and `apply --all` then marks a live application rejected. Triggers: "Due to the high volume of applications, unfortunately we are unable to respond to every applicant individually"; "Unfortunately, we can't provide individual feedback at this stage"; "unfortunately we need to move Thursday's call". All come back `rejection`; matched to an applied role the proposal has no warnings. Also "The Head of Platform position has been filled, but your application for Engineering Manager remains under review" is classed rejection. Expected: `unfortunately,? we` is far too broad. A message that also hits an acknowledgement phrase must be `ambiguous` (never proposed), and that phrase alone must not decide a rejection. A `position has been filled` hit alongside `remains under review` / `still under review` must not be a rejection.

## HIGH
2. mailsync.py:368 and 386-412, warnings at 261-264. PROVEN. `apply --all` can move a rejection back to interviewing: a payload in Graph's newest-first order (rejection 10-05 first, interview invite 10-01 second) gets proposal ids in payload order, warnings were computed while the role was still `applied`, so both are clean; applying in id order sets rejected then interviewing. Also an interview mail for a role already at `rejected` gets no warning and `--all` moves it rejected to interviewing. Expected: apply in `happens_at` order, recompute warnings at apply time against the CURRENT status, and never let `--all` move a status backwards (progress order in store.PROGRESS). A backwards move needs an explicit id and a visible warning.
3. mailsync.py:391, `uid = r["uid"] or role_uid`. PROVEN. `mail-sync apply ID --role X` ignores `--role` when the proposal already has a role (proposal wrongly matched to Monarch, `apply 1 --role Acme` marks Monarch rejected, prints "applied 1"). Expected: explicit `--role` overrides, or the command refuses; never silently applies to a different role than the one named.
4. deadlines.py:114-115 and :31. PROVEN. (a) Latest date in the 60-character window wins: "Closing date: 23 October 2026. Interviews: 6 November 2026." stores 2026-11-06 (should be 2026-10-23); "Closing date: 23rd October 2026 (interviews w/c 2 November)" stores 2026-11-02; "Apply by 20 October 2026; start date 4 January 2027." stores 2027-01-04. (b) `closes?\b` matches "close" as a strong cue: "Join a close-knit team. Posted 1 September 2026." stores 2026-09-01; "close partnership ... Start date: 5 January 2027" stores 2027-01-05. Expected: take the date NEAREST the cue (first after it); the "latest wins" rule only for an explicit range "X - Y" / "X to Y" joined by a range word; drop the bare `close`/`closes?` cue in favour of `closes on|closes at|closes:|closing`, and require that the cue not be followed by a hyphen+word ("close-knit"). Also `backfill_closing_dates` never overwrites a set date, so values already written by a buggy parser stay: add a way to re-derive only dates whose `closes_evidence` is non-empty (parser-written, not "by hand") so a fixed parser can correct them.
5. cvcheck.py:126-129, 143. PROVEN. The banned-claim check misses a claim wrapped onto a second line ("two engineers now\nrun workstreams" reports PASS even with a `\s+` pattern). PDF text wraps at the visual line, so this is the normal case. Expected: match the whole text with whitespace collapsed to single spaces, then map the hit back to a line number for display.
6. importer.py:73-76, used at 107-109 and 115. PROVEN. Moves an upcoming interview date back a year: status interviewing, note "Interview 12 Oct", updated_at 2026-10-08 gives applied_on 2025-10-12 and an interviewing event dated 2025-10-12, then the duplicate guard says "was already applied for on 2025-10-12". With "Applied 30 Sep ... Interview 12 Oct" the interview is filed a year before the application. "offer 20 Oct" becomes 2025-10-20. Expected: interview and offer dates may be in the future; never cap them at `updated_at`, never derive an `applied_on` estimate from them, and resolve a year-less day/month to the occurrence nearest `updated_at` (within roughly plus or minus 6 months), not by capping.

## MEDIUM
7. cli.py:2350-2354. PROVEN. Re-running `applied <uid>` (or with `--contact`) on a role at `rejected` that already has an application sets the status back to `applied` while printing "already recorded" / "nothing was changed". There is also no way to add a contact to an existing application, so imported applications can never be followed up (followups need a contact). Expected: a status change is never silent and never goes backwards without the explicit `-s`; add contact/route/reference/cv/cover/salary updating of an existing application (fill blanks, or `--set` overwrite) and print what changed.

9. store.py:975-977 (UPDATE OR IGNORE then DELETE). PROVEN. Expected: fill blanks on the kept row from the losing row; for events, keep the longer detail.
10. Estimated dates shown as known. PROVEN. `describe_prior` (store.py:887) prints "was already applied for on X" with no ESTIMATED label, in the CLI and the dashboard refusal; the interview pack (interviewprep.py:131) prints "Applied on: X" likewise. importer.py:107-109 and :220: when a note states the applied date but not the final date, the final event is dated from `updated_at` yet filed under source `import:note`. Expected: an application row needs to carry whether its date is estimated (add an additive column or derive it from source `import:updated_at` and the event source); every message that prints a date from such a row says "about X (estimated from when the status last changed)"; the final event's source follows where ITS date came from.
11. mailsync.py:252, store.py:860. PROVEN. A "You already applied for the job" acknowledgement on a role at status `new` applies cleanly with no warning, status stays `new`, no application is recorded, and `duplicate_refusal` returns None: the Brightwell duplicate again. `rescreen --remove` only spares roles with an `applications` row (cli.py ~3040) so it would delete that event (READ-ONLY for the delete). Expected: an acknowledgement or duplicate-refused message for a role with no application record proposes recording one (event kind acknowledged, plus an application row dated from the message, source `mail:<id>`), with the warning "role is not recorded as applied".
12. mailsync.py:173-180. PROVEN. Companies named with common words take over the match (sources include Next, HERE, Today, Link): subject "Next steps on your application", body "Thank you for your interest in Acme. Unfortunately..." attributes Acme's rejection to Next. Expected: a company name that is a common English word (build a short stop list: next, here, today, link, apple?, no: keep it to words that are ordinary English AND short, plus any single-word name of 6 letters or fewer) matches only when it appears capitalised as a name AND NOT at the start of a sentence in a phrase from a fixed set ("next steps", "link to"), or in the sender's domain label; otherwise it is an `ambiguous company` proposal with candidates. A sender-domain match outranks a subject match.
13. Duplicate guard over-blocks and the dashboard cannot override. PROVEN. "Senior Engineering Manager, Platform" is refused because "Senior Engineering Manager, Payments" at the same company was rejected (titles share 3 words), and a rejected role blocks forever. The dashboard JS never sends `force`, so the "force: true" message cannot be acted on. Expected: a SIBLING (different uid) refusal only when the titles are the same job (normalised titles equal, or one contains the other after removing seniority words), not on a 3-word overlap, and the dashboard refusal gets a visible "Draft anyway" action that re-sends with force true. A refusal for the SAME role stays.
14. runner.py:1570. READ-ONLY. `regate` carries `reviewed`/`review_findings` across edits to CV.md with nothing tying the verdict to the text reviewed, so a passing review stays on a CV later edited to add an unsupported claim. Expected: store a hash of the reviewed text (add to the gates dict as `reviewed_hash`) and `regate` drops/marks the review as stale (`reviewed: False`, finding "stale: document changed since it was reviewed") when the hash no longer matches.

READ-ONLY. `--allowedTools Read` ADDS to the user's settings, it does not restrict them; Read takes absolute paths so an empty working directory is not isolation; if the user's settings allow Write/Edit/Bash the reviewer can write into the role folder, contradicting the docstring. Expected: also pass `--disallowedTools Write Edit NotebookEdit Bash` (check the exact flag names with `claude --help`, and verify the behaviour with a harmless probe if you can without spending tokens, otherwise state what you could not verify), and tell the reviewer prompt that the posting text is untrusted data and cannot change the output format. LOW addition: reject a reviewer reply whose JSON has extra keys or whose lists are not lists of strings, as `unmeasured`.
16. deadlines.py:86-88 via store backfill. PROVEN. A year-less closing date still rolls a year forward for stale postings: role first seen 2026-10-08 whose text says "Closing date: 30th September" stores 2027-09-30; the dashboard shows "Closes 30 Sep" with no year (interactive.py `_short_date`) and shows past dates as "Closes" rather than "Closed". Expected: resolve a year-less date against the role's `first_seen` (the day the posting was seen), choosing the occurrence on or after `first_seen - 7 days`; when the result is more than 400 days after `first_seen`, treat as ambiguous (leave blank). The dashboard shows "Closed 30 Sep" for a past date and includes the year when it is not the current year.
